# T00 — Domain Knowledge

## 1. Domain Overview

**Project**: ShadeTree.IT LLC business website  
**Domain**: Practical technology, operations, data, and leadership consulting for small businesses  
**Last updated / research date**: November 16, 2026

**Bottom line**: Owners need help improving actual work, not another abstract technology pitch. AI adoption is uneven; useful assistance includes choosing an appropriate task, preparing information, training people, checking outputs, and maintaining the resulting workflow. ShadeTree.IT can credibly connect technical work with how owners and teams operate, but that positioning is a hypothesis—not evidence of customer results.

**Research brief**: Understand new and established small-business owners equally; how they buy practical help; AI adoption and oversight; process/software/data dependencies; leadership support; and digital-presence fundamentals. Inform clear, trustworthy service descriptions and a simple contact journey. Exclude market sizing, geographic targeting, industry specialization, pricing strategy, an SEO campaign, and changes to approved product decisions.

**Evidence convention**: “Fact” refers to the cited source; “interpretation” is a reasoned implication; “recommendation” is proposed guidance. Workflow examples are illustrative discovery patterns, not observed ShadeTree.IT engagements or promised deliverables.

## 2. Market Context

- **Fact — adoption is not universal**: Census BTOS analysis of December 14, 2025–May 3, 2026 reports overall business AI use around 17–20%, with fewer than 20% of firms with four or fewer employees reporting use. Its November 2025 question changed from use in producing goods/services to use in any business function. These figures are period-specific, not a November 2026 estimate, and should not be compared uncritically with older surveys. [S1]
- **Fact — adoption involves operational change**: The Census supplement studies training, workflow adjustment, and technology investment, alongside AI use across functions such as finance, customer service, and marketing. This supports examining the work surrounding a tool rather than equating subscriptions with successful adoption. [S1]
- **Fact — guidance already exists at low or no cost**: SBA offers planning/management guidance and access to counseling/resource partners for starting and growing businesses. A consultant competes with accessible advice, not just other consultants. [S2]
- **Fact — connecting tools is not the same as eliminating maintenance**: Zapier documents triggers/actions, failed runs, disconnected applications, task limits, history, and replay. Microsoft documents reporting refresh failures caused by credentials and source-schema changes. These are concrete operational dependencies, not evidence that either product is unsuitable. [S3, S4]
- **Interpretation**: Buyers may reasonably choose an existing software feature, a better spreadsheet, self-directed training, or no change. Custom work needs a reason beyond “we can build it.”

**Relevant substitutes and competitors**:

- **DIY and existing tools**: Email, spreadsheets, accounting/CRM features, built-in AI assistants, and vendor tutorials. Familiarity and low switching effort matter; fragmented information and maintenance can remain.
- **Automation and reporting platforms**: Zapier-style integrations and Power BI-style reporting may solve a bounded need without custom software. Vendor documentation establishes capabilities and dependencies, not comparative ROI. [S3, S4]
- **Human alternatives**: SBA/resource-partner counseling, software vendors, specialist developers, automation consultants, web providers, and executive coaches. This is a category map, not a ranked or exhaustive competitor study. [S2, S5]

## 3. User Landscape

These are research-informed buyer hypotheses to validate, not customer interview findings.

### Owner starting a business

- **Goal**: Establish workable routines and a credible digital presence without overbuilding.
- **Friction**: Limited operating history; competing choices about tools, costs, customers, and priorities.
- **Buying priorities**: Plain explanations, a manageable starting point, clear ownership, and help the owner can continue using.
- **Research basis**: SBA separates planning/startup work from managing and growing operations. [S2]

### Owner of an established business

- **Goal**: Reduce repetitive work, understand performance, and make growth less dependent on personal intervention.
- **Friction**: Existing tools and habits; inconsistent information; staff handoffs; risk of interrupting work.
- **Buying priorities**: Fit with current operations, a bounded change, reliable recovery when something fails, and a named person responsible for follow-through.
- **Research basis**: Integration failures and reporting-refresh dependencies demonstrate why operating continuity matters; they do not establish how frequently this audience experiences these problems. [S3, S4]

### Staff member or working manager

- **Goal**: Complete real tasks correctly with less re-entry and confusion.
- **Friction**: Training time, unclear exceptions, changing tools, and uncertainty about which information can enter AI systems.
- **Buying influence**: Usually not the final buyer, but adoption depends on whether the changed process is understandable and useful. Human oversight and information protection require explicit responsibilities. [S6, S7]

### Leader receiving coaching

- **Goal**: Improve judgment, delegation, communication, or accountability.
- **Friction**: Sensitive conversations and unclear boundaries when the buyer/sponsor is someone else.
- **Priority**: Understand whether the engagement supplies advice, teaches a skill, or supports the leader’s own reflection—and what will remain confidential. ICF offers a useful professional benchmark, not evidence of Adam’s membership or credentials. [S5]

## 4. Domain Workflows & Decision Points

1. **Select a practical AI use case**: Owner/staff identify a repeated task, describe its inputs and quality requirements, assess information sensitivity, and try a bounded example. Compare reviewed output with the existing approach; document when not to use the tool. Success means acceptable results and an understood review responsibility—not simply a compelling demo. **Recommendation**, grounded in NIST’s lifecycle risk approach and FTC data guidance. [S6, S7]
2. **Improve a back-office process**: Trace an illustrative inquiry → quote → work → invoice handoff; identify re-entry, approval delays, and exceptions. Decide whether to simplify the process, configure current tools, connect them, or write a custom tool. Validate an ordinary case and a failed/duplicate case; assign ongoing ownership. **Interpretation**, supported by documented automation failure/recovery mechanisms. [S3]
3. **Turn business data into a decision**: Begin with an owner’s question; identify source records; agree what the measures mean; clean and reconcile a sample; establish refresh and access; review whether the result changes an action. Success is a usable, trusted answer rather than a dashboard alone. **Recommendation**; source-schema and credential dependencies are documented facts. [S4]
4. **Build leadership capability**: Agree the objective and distinguish teaching a skill from advising on a situation or coaching toward the client’s own decisions. Establish confidentiality and any sponsor reporting, practice an action, and revisit progress. **Recommendation**, informed by ICF’s definition and agreement standards; no certification implied. [S5]
5. **Establish a digital footprint**: Clarify who the business helps, what it does, who is responsible, and how to make contact. Publish accessible, crawlable, descriptive content and accurate metadata. Search discoverability is a process, not a guaranteed ranking or lead count. **Recommendation**, consistent with Google’s starter guidance. [S8]
6. **Choose a consultant and scope the work**: Recognize a problem → check identity and relevant capabilities → contact the person → discuss current tools, constraints, and desired outcome → agree a bounded scope and responsibilities. **Buyer-journey hypothesis**, not a sourced finding about ShadeTree.IT conversions.

**Decision points that matter**:

- Does this need AI, deterministic automation, a process change, training, or no new technology?
- Can current software do enough before adding subscriptions, integrations, or custom code?
- What data may be accessed, by whom, under what terms, and with what retention?
- What must a person approve before an output affects customers, money, or staff?
- Who owns accounts, source material, documentation, exceptions, and maintenance?
- What observable outcome would justify continuing after an initial bounded effort?

## 5. Market Expectations & Differentiation

### What users already expect

- **Fact**: Widely available platforms already offer automation and reporting; “connect your apps” or “build a dashboard” alone does not distinguish a consultant. [S3, S4]
- **Interpretation**: Clear service language, visible identity, direct contact, and honest boundaries reduce uncertainty for a buyer considering a personal service.
- **Recommendation**: Describe outcomes with recognizable examples, but never turn illustrative scenarios into implied client case studies, quantified savings, or guaranteed results.
- **Fact**: Information security is not reserved for large companies; FTC guidance recommends knowing what data exists, minimizing it, protecting it, disposing of it appropriately, and planning for incidents. [S7]

### Where this project can differentiate

- **Positioning hypothesis**: Join practical AI consulting/training with process improvement and data work, so the conversation can start with the business problem rather than a prescribed tool.
- **Positioning hypothesis**: A named owner who can explain technical choices in business terms may feel more approachable than an anonymous agency presentation. This requires validation, not a superiority claim.
- **Recommendation**: Preserve four clear service groups: AI consulting/training; custom software and process improvement; data/reporting/insights; leadership training/executive coaching. Treat websites/digital footprint/basic SEO as an additional offering, not a fifth competing headline.
- **Recommendation**: Keep the approved promise—“Less busywork. Better decisions. More room to grow.”—as an aspiration supported by concrete services, not a measurable performance guarantee.

### Risks to the current thesis

- Breadth can obscure what to hire Adam for; concise grouping helps but does not prove demand.
- Existing features, inexpensive subscriptions, and public counseling can be sufficient.
- AI interest does not demonstrate willingness to pay for consulting or training.
- Owner-led delivery creates capacity and continuity questions to settle in scoping.
- No primary customer interviews, conversion data, pricing research, or permissioned client outcomes were available. Do not invent proof to fill that gap.

## 6. Regulatory / Operational Constraints

- **Information protection**: Inventory client/customer/employee data before tool selection; minimize collection, restrict access, review vendor terms, and agree retention. Do not place confidential source material in an AI service merely because it accepts uploads. FTC identifies privacy/confidentiality promises as consequential and warns that AI services can receive sensitive business information. [S7, S9]
- **AI oversight**: NIST’s AI RMF is voluntary guidance, not a certification. Its lifecycle approach and generative-AI profile support assessing risks and defining evaluation/oversight appropriate to the task. The landing page notes RMF 1.0 is being revised; do not portray it as an immutable current requirement. [S6]
- **Engagement-specific obligations**: Industry, data types, contracts, client locations, and proposed uses determine applicable legal requirements. No universal compliance claim, sector certification, or jurisdictional coverage has been established. Seek appropriate legal/security advice before regulated work; this research is not legal advice.
- **Coaching boundaries**: Explain roles, confidentiality limits, and sponsor reporting before an engagement. Use ICF standards as an external benchmark only; do not imply ICF or EOS certification, affiliation, or a public EOS offering. [S5]
- **Maintenance**: Credentials expire, integrations fail, usage limits apply, and source structures change. Support expectations and account ownership belong in a private scope, not an unqualified “set and forget” promise. [S3, S4]
- **Website boundary — approved constraint**: Static Astro/TypeScript, GitHub Pages/custom domain, direct phone/email; no forms, tracking, analytics, cookies, embeds, booking, or client-data portal. This limits site-side collection, but email/phone conversations and hosting-provider processing still exist. Avoid a blanket “no data is collected” claim.
- **Public claims — approved constraint**: Use ShadeTree.IT LLC and Adam Jelinek — Owner; no invented credentials, results, employer/client names, prices, free-call promises, or geographic positioning. U.S. sources inform operational context; they do not restrict the audience geographically.

## 7. Domain Vocabulary & Concepts

- **AI consulting**: Choosing and applying AI appropriately to a business task; not necessarily training a custom model.
- **AI training**: Teaching people effective use, limitations, information handling, and output review; distinct from training a machine-learning model.
- **Automation**: Defined work executed after an event or schedule; it need not involve AI.
- **Workflow / handoff**: Steps and transfers of responsibility required to complete work, including exceptions.
- **Integration / custom tool**: Connecting existing systems versus building behavior current tools do not adequately supply.
- **Data pipeline**: Repeatable movement and preparation of information from its source toward a usable destination.
- **Reporting / business insight**: Reporting presents agreed information; insight interprets what it means for an action. Neither implies guaranteed predictive accuracy.
- **Source of truth / data quality**: An agreed authoritative record and the fitness of information for a decision.
- **Human oversight**: A named person with the ability and responsibility to check, reject, or correct consequential outputs.
- **Consulting / training / coaching**: Advising or delivering expertise / teaching a skill / supporting the client’s own development and decisions. These can coexist, but the role should be clear. The coaching distinction is informed by ICF’s definition. [S5]
- **Digital footprint / basic SEO**: Accurate, coherent public presence and technical/content fundamentals that help people and search engines understand it—not guaranteed placement or a marketing campaign.

## 8. Implications For This Project

- **For `101-project-overview`**: Record the approved owner-centered value proposition and equal inclusion of new and established businesses. Separate known capabilities from unvalidated demand and differentiation hypotheses; do not restart the approved interview.
- **For architecture and data**: Keep this project a public information/contact site, not a consulting delivery platform. No client records, AI execution, reporting backend, lead database, or unnecessary integration follows from the services being described.
- **For UX and UI**: Prefer understandable outcomes over “AI transformation” language. Introduce the actual person; keep phone/email obvious; give services enough specificity for self-identification. Personal warmth and polished craft are approved direction, not a research-proven conversion mechanism.
- **For feature planning**: Prioritize accurate service content, a concise credible bio, working direct-contact links, mobile/accessibility fundamentals, and strong sharing metadata. Do not add forms, scheduling, a feed, case studies, metrics, or tracking to imitate agency templates.
- **For later engagement discovery**: Ask about a real task, current systems, sensitive information, responsible staff, failure recovery, and success criteria. These are private scoping topics, not mandatory extra website sections.
